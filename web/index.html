A firewall and flight recorder for MCP

Cryptographic authority over your autonomous agents.

Sceau sits between your AI agents and the tools they call. It stops what they should not do, waits for a person when a decision is theirs, and keeps a signed record your client's security team can check on their own.

The policy

Rules a person can read aloud.

Plain YAML, checked when Sceau starts: a rule it cannot read stops it from starting, it never falls through to allow. The first rule that matches decides. SQL is read for what it would do, so a delete hidden behind a select does not slip past, and an address that came out of your own CRM is told apart from one the agent made up.

- name: nothing is ever deleted
  match: crm/query
  if: [{ sql: [delete, drop, truncate] }]
  then: deny
- name: the agent reads the database freely
  match: crm/query
  if: [{ sql_only: [select] }]
  then: allow
- name: refunds above 200 EUR wait for finance
  match: payments/refund
  if: [{ arg: amount, gt: 200 }]
  then: hold
- name: bank details never leave by mail
  match: mail/send_email
  if: [{ carries: [iban, card] }]
  then: deny

Before the model reads it

The model gets a token.
The tool gets the IBAN.

Results are scanned on their way back to the agent. IBANs are checked against their country's length and checksum, cards against Luhn, keys against the shapes the big providers use. Each value becomes a stable token; when the agent hands a token to a tool, Sceau puts the real value back. The table between the two lives in memory and is never written down.

And what it should never read

A supplier's invoice with a comment that says ignore previous instructions, white text in a PDF, Unicode tag characters that render as nothing: tool results that try to steer the model are withheld and recorded with what they said. Tool descriptions are fingerprinted too, so a server that rewrites a tool after you trusted it shows up in the ledger the moment it does.

Human in the loop

Some calls wait for a hand on the seal.

A held call stays open on the agent's side while a person decides, in the console or in Slack. A click is too easy to give by mistake, so sealing is a press held for less than a second. Whoever seals, and when, goes into the record next to the call.

The black box

A record that cannot be quietly edited.

One JSON line per event, written to disk before the call goes on. Each line carries the SHA-256 of its content and of the line before it, and an Ed25519 signature. Change a number, drop an entry, reorder two, and every check after it fails, at the exact line.

Evidence pack

One click in the console exports any range as a zip your client's auditor opens without installing anything: the ledger slice, the public key, the policy in force, a plain report of who decided what, and a manifest signed over all of it.

  • ledger.jsonlthe entries, as written
  • manifest.jsonrange, first and last hash, a digest per file
  • manifest.sigEd25519 over the manifest's exact bytes
  • report.htmldecisions, refusals, masked data, policy
openssl pkeyutl -verify -pubin -inkey sceau.pub -rawin -in manifest.json -sigfile manifest.sig

Built with the EU AI Act in mind: automatic records of events (Article 12) and people able to step in and stop a system (Article 14). Sceau produces the evidence. It does not, on its own, make anyone compliant.

Run it

One container in front of your tools.

HTTP tool servers go behind /mcp/<name>. Local ones that speak over stdio are started through sceau wrap, which hands every message to the proxy and refuses calls if the proxy is gone. Nothing leaves your network: no account, no telemetry, the key and the ledger stay in one folder you own.
